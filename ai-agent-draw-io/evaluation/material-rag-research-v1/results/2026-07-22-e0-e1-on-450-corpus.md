# E0 & E1 re-run on the 450 corpus (dataset v-next), development, dense-only

Re-run of E0 (canonical-v4, no paragraph merge) and E1 (canonical-v5, paragraph
merge) on the frozen **450** core corpus. Only the chunking variable changes
between E0 and E1; everything else (corpus, split, embedding, budgets, gates) is
identical. Development split now yields **155 tested cases** (vs 62 on the 240
corpus), so slices are far more trustworthy.

## Headline: E1 improvement is larger and cleaner on the bigger corpus

| Metric | E0 (v4) | E1 (v5) | Δ | V2 gate |
|---|--:|--:|--:|:--:|
| Recall@1 | 0.503 | 0.593 | +0.090 | — |
| Recall@5 | 0.729 | 0.871 | +0.142 | — |
| Recall@10 | 0.748 | **0.897** | +0.148 | ≥0.90 (0.897, just under) |
| Recall@40 | 0.800 | **0.955** | +0.155 | ≥0.95 ✅ |
| MRR@10 | 0.601 | 0.715 | +0.114 | ≥0.75 (under) |
| indexed chunks | 920 | 533 | −387 | — |

Notes:
- The 450 E0 baseline (0.748) is **lower** than the 240 E0 baseline (0.839)
  because the larger corpus is harder: more documents, more near-miss distractors,
  920 vs 724 chunks. This is a more realistic baseline.
- E1's gain here is **+0.148 R@10** vs **+0.065** on the 240 corpus — the
  paragraph-merge effect is bigger, not smaller, on the more realistic dataset.
  E1 clears Recall@40 (0.955) and lands just under Recall@10 (0.897); the last
  bit is for retrieval-side experiments (E3 hybrid / E5 rerank), not more merging.

## The multi_evidence "regression" was noise

On the 240 corpus (n=9) E1 appeared to regress multi_evidence 0.778 → 0.667, and
we flagged it as a watch item. On the 450 corpus (n=20) multi_evidence **improves
0.550 → 0.850** — the largest slice gain. So the earlier dip was small-sample
noise, exactly the risk the dataset-v-next expansion was meant to remove. E1
promotes on the bigger corpus with **no slice regression**.

## Verdict

- E0 (450) is the new frozen dense baseline for the v-next corpus: R@10 0.748,
  R@40 0.800, MRR 0.601.
- **E1 (paragraph merge) promotes cleanly**: +0.148 R@10, clears the R@40 gate,
  every slice improves, no regression. The remaining gap to the R@10/MRR gates is
  a retrieval-side problem (E3 hybrid), not a representation one.
- Methodological payoff: the 240→450 expansion made per-slice conclusions
  trustworthy and overturned a noise-driven caveat.
